# ASTRO CONSTRUCTION - CONTENT RULES

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the writing rules for all user-facing ASTRO CONSTRUCTION website content.

The goal is professional construction content that:

- sounds natural in Latvian;
- is understandable to an ordinary adult without construction education;
- remains technically correct;
- avoids bureaucratic and AI-style wording;
- explains practical meaning before legal detail;
- gives exact documents, roles, actions, conditions and results;
- supports trust, SEO, GEO/AEO and conversion without sounding like marketing copy.

These rules apply to:

- service pages;
- construction term pages;
- BIS pages;
- FAQ;
- expert pages;
- process explanations;
- legal/regulatory explanations;
- commercial page copy;
- forms and helper text;
- navigation labels where relevant.

Project Instructions and approved user-facing copy remain higher priority.

## 2. Core writing principle

Write like an experienced Latvian construction specialist speaking normally to a client.

Do not write like:

- legislation;
- a municipality;
- a lawyer;
- a textbook;
- a generic SEO article;
- an AI-generated explainer;
- a corporate brochure.

A 25-year-old non-professional should understand the text without losing technical correctness.

Use professional terms when they are the correct terms. Explain them in ordinary language when the reader may not know them.

## 3. Start with practical meaning

Lead with the answer or practical meaning.

For construction, BIS and regulatory topics, explain first:

- who does what;
- what exact document is prepared;
- what is submitted;
- where it is submitted;
- who checks it;
- who signs it;
- when work may start;
- what happens next;
- what the client receives at the end.

Legal references, regulation numbers and formal terminology come later unless the legal reference itself is the user’s question.

Do not start with abstract definitions if the practical meaning can be stated directly.

## 4. One sentence - one main idea

Prefer short direct sentences.

Each sentence should normally communicate one main idea.

Avoid sentences with several abstract clauses joined only to sound complete or formal.

Good content should be easy to scan and easy to extract by both people and search/answer engines.

## 5. Every sentence must add information

Remove:

- filler;
- decorative transitions;
- repeated conclusions;
- vague summaries;
- statements added only for rhythm;
- sentences that merely rephrase the previous sentence;
- artificial endings added to make a section feel complete.

Stop when the useful information ends.

Do not lengthen content for symmetry.

Do not force equal-length sections.

## 6. Concrete language

Use exact nouns and verbs.

Prefer:

- "iesniedz būvvaldē";
- "paraksta būvdarbu vadītājs";
- "pievieno BIS lietai";
- "sagatavo izpildmērījumu";
- "būvvalde pārbauda";
- "pasūtītājs saņem aktu";

instead of abstract wording such as:

- "veic nepieciešamās darbības";
- "nodrošina atbilstošu procesu";
- "iesniedz attiecīgo dokumentāciju";
- "ievēro noteiktās prasības".

If a sentence leaves one of these questions unanswered, rewrite it:

- ko tieši?
- kādu dokumentu?
- kas tieši dara?
- kad?
- kur?
- kurā gadījumā?
- kas tieši mainās?
- ko cilvēks saņem beigās?

## 7. No generalisations

Never replace known specific information with an umbrella term.

If the exact composition is verified, write it in full.

Instead of "dokumenti", name the documents.

Instead of "darbi", name the works.

Instead of "darbības", name the actions.

Instead of "iesaistītās personas", name the roles.

Instead of "process", state the steps.

Instead of "prasības", state the exact requirements.

Do not use:

- "utt.";
- "u.c.";
- "un citi";
- similar endings

to avoid listing known contents.

If composition depends on a condition, state the condition and give the full verified composition for that condition.

If something is not verified, say exactly what remains unconfirmed.

## 8. Forbidden vague wording

Avoid vague substitutes such as:

- noteiktos;
- atsevišķos;
- konkrētos;
- dažos gadījumos;
- atkarībā no situācijas;
- vajadzības gadījumā;
- ja nepieciešams;
- var būt;
- attiecīgais;
- attiecīgā;
- attiecīgie;
- attiecīgās;
- atbilstošs;
- atbilstoša;
- atbilstoši;
- atbilstošās;
- ņemot vērā.

Do not mechanically replace one vague word with another.

Rewrite the sentence with the exact condition, action, document, role or result.

## 9. Forbidden empty labels

Do not use empty labels such as:

- prasītie dokumenti;
- vajadzīgie dokumenti;
- nepieciešamie dokumenti;
- jāsagatavo nepieciešamā dokumentācija;
- pārējie dokumenti;
- citi dokumenti;
- nepieciešamie pasākumi;
- nepieciešamās darbības;
- paredzētie darbi;
- attiecīgā dokumentācija.

Name the actual contents instead.

## 10. Strictly forbidden constructions

Do not use these constructions in user-facing ASTRO content:

- atbilstošas darbības sfēras;
- attiecīgās prasības;
- nepieciešamie dokumenti;
- paredzētie darbi;
- konkrētajā situācijā;
- atkarībā no apstākļiem;
- veikt nepieciešamās darbības;
- nodrošināt atbilstošu risinājumu;
- ievērot noteiktās prasības;
- iesniegt attiecīgo dokumentāciju;
- prasītos dokumentus;
- piemēram.

`Piemēram` is intentionally excluded from visible ASTRO website copy under the current approved style direction. If an example is useful, state the real situation directly instead of introducing it with a generic example marker.

Do not replace these expressions with equivalent vague wording.

State exactly what is meant.

## 11. Forbidden style templates

Do not use stylistic negative-contrast templates such as:

- Tas nav...
- Šis nav...
- Šī nav...
- nav tikai...
- X nav tikai Y, bet arī Z...
- Runa nav par...
- Nevis X, bet Y...
- Patiesībā...
- Īstenībā...
- X nenozīmē Y...
- Ar X vien nepietiek...
- Nepietiek tikai ar...
- Nevajag jaukt X ar Y...

The phrase "nav tikai" is forbidden regardless of what comes before it.

Normal factual negation is allowed when needed to state a fact accurately.

Rewrite direct positive meaning instead.

## 12. Punctuation and visual style

Do not use long dashes `—` or `–` in user-facing website copy.

Use normal punctuation or a hyphen `-` where appropriate.

Avoid decorative punctuation.

Avoid excessive semicolons.

Avoid headline structures that sound like advertising slogans if a direct title works better.

## 13. Tone

The tone should be:

- professional;
- calm;
- direct;
- practical;
- clear.

Do not sound:

- ceremonial;
- bureaucratic;
- overconfident;
- salesy;
- defensive;
- legalistic without need;
- academic.

Avoid generic claims such as:

- profesionāla komanda;
- individuāla pieeja;
- augsta kvalitāte;
- pilns serviss;
- kompleksais risinājums

unless the page provides a concrete reason to use the phrase and a more exact statement would not be better.

Prefer facts over self-praise.

## 14. Benefits

Use concrete benefits only when they are useful and supported.

Approved direct benefit wording may include:

- ātrāk;
- vienkāršāk;
- mazāk dokumentu;
- mazāks laiks līdz būvniecības uzsākšanai;
- zemākas izmaksas.

Do not hedge a verified practical benefit unnecessarily.

Do not invent:

- time savings;
- cost savings;
- percentages;
- deadlines;
- guarantees;
- success rates.

If a benefit depends on a defined condition, state that condition.

## 15. Commercial pages

Commercial pages should lead with:

- what ASTRO does;
- what problem it solves;
- what the client receives;
- what the next step is.

Do not start with internal mechanics when the client first needs to understand the result.

Move detailed legal, regulatory and process information lower on the page when that improves clarity.

Hide internal operational detail when it has no value for the client.

Do not turn an informational page into advertising copy.

Do not add CTA text mechanically to every section.

## 16. Construction terminology pages

Answer the real user question, not only the dictionary definition.

A term page should usually explain:

1. practical meaning;
2. when it is used;
3. who prepares or signs it;
4. exact documents or actions involved;
5. what happens next;
6. what the client receives;
7. real limitation or exception when it changes the action.

Do not force the same exact section structure onto every term if another structure is clearer.

Use "Kas tas ir?" when useful.

Use "Svarīgi" only for:

- a real limitation;
- a real exception;
- a transition rule;
- an obsolete term;
- a condition that changes what the user must do.

Do not use "Svarīgi" merely to emphasize ordinary content.

## 17. Hooks for construction terms

Each term should begin with a practical hook, usually 8-14 words.

The hook should state the main meaning or action.

It should not be clickbait.

Example direction:

"Būvatļauja - būvvalde atļauj sākt konkrēta objekta būvniecību."

The hook should help the reader understand the term before reading detail.

## 18. BIS content

For BIS-related pages, prioritize practical actions.

Explain:

- who creates the entry;
- who uploads the file;
- who signs;
- where in BIS it appears;
- what status or next step follows;
- when another role must act;
- what the entry/document proves.

Do not describe BIS only as an abstract digital system.

Use exact BIS document names where verified.

Do not invent menu names, statuses or workflow steps.

If the current BIS behavior has not been verified, state that it remains unconfirmed.

## 19. Roles

Use exact professional roles internally and in technical analysis.

In visible ASTRO website copy, the approved terminology restriction is stricter:

- do not use `ierosinātājs`, including `būvniecības ierosinātājs`;
- do not use `būvdarbu veicējs`, including `galvenais būvdarbu veicējs`.

Use the concrete natural-language role approved for that page and situation.

Other professional role names may be used when they are technically correct, including:

- būvdarbu vadītājs;
- būvuzraugs;
- autoruzraugs;
- projektētājs;
- būvvalde;
- būvinspektors;
- pasūtītājs.

Do not generalize several distinct roles into `iesaistītās personas` when the exact roles are known.

If the exact natural-language replacement for a prohibited formal role has not been approved, do not invent one. Flag the wording for content review.

## 20. Legal and regulatory accuracy

Accuracy is mandatory.

Before publishing legal, BIS, regulatory or technical statements, verify:

- document name;
- responsible role;
- submission order;
- signing order;
- trigger condition;
- deadline;
- exception;
- current regulation status.

Never invent deadlines, mandatory documents or exceptions.

If a rule changed over time, state the relevant date or transition condition.

If only part of the rule is confirmed, say exactly what is confirmed and what is not.

## 21. Prices

Do not invent prices.

When a verified ASTRO price is used:

- state whether VAT is included;
- state the unit or scope;
- state if the price is "from" a value;
- distinguish internal cost from client price;
- distinguish ASTRO service price from a state fee or third-party fee.

Do not present an approximate state fee as a fixed ASTRO price.

## 22. Deadlines and timing

Do not invent duration.

When timing is known, state:

- who controls it;
- what starts the clock;
- what can stop or extend it;
- whether the period is statutory, contractual, practical or estimated.

Avoid vague phrases like:

- "process may take some time";
- "depending on circumstances";
- "within the required time".

Use the exact verified period or state that the period has not been confirmed.

## 23. Documents

When listing documents:

- use the exact document name;
- keep naming consistent throughout the page;
- state who prepares it where relevant;
- state who signs it where relevant;
- state where it is submitted or stored where relevant;
- state when it is required.

Do not merge distinct documents under one broad heading if the distinction affects user action.

## 24. Processes

When explaining a process, write actual steps.

Prefer:

1. client provides X;
2. ASTRO checks Y;
3. architect prepares Z;
4. document is submitted in BIS;
5. authority reviews it;
6. after approval, the next action is X.

Do not call something a "process" and then describe only general intentions.

Use step order only when the order is verified.

## 25. Examples

Use examples when they make a technical rule understandable.

Examples should be realistic.

Do not invent ASTRO clients, objects or completed cases.

If a hypothetical example is used, make clear that it is an example.

Prefer ordinary construction situations:

- existing house;
- reconstruction;
- demolition;
- extension;
- undocumented works;
- hidden condition discovered during reconstruction;
- missing execution documentation;
- commissioning.

## 26. Evidence language

Use concrete evidence names.

Prefer:

- photo;
- drawing;
- execution scheme;
- protocol;
- BIS entry;
- measurement;
- act;
- technical conclusion;
- survey;
- inspection record.

Avoid abstract wording like "pierādījumi" when exact evidence can be named.

## 27. SEO language

Write for people first.

SEO must not make the text unnatural.

Do not:

- repeat keywords mechanically;
- force exact-match phrases into every paragraph;
- write artificial FAQ solely for keywords;
- add filler sections for word count;
- create near-duplicate wording across related pages.

Use natural query language when it matches how people actually search.

## 28. GEO/AEO language

Make important answers extractable.

Prefer explicit statements with:

- named document;
- named role;
- named action;
- clear condition;
- direct result.

Avoid pronouns when they make the answer ambiguous.

Do not hide the core answer in a long introduction.

Where useful, answer the likely user question in the first sentence.

## 29. FAQ rules

FAQ should contain real questions.

Each answer should:

- answer the question immediately;
- add information that is not already obvious from the question;
- stay concise unless detail changes the action;
- avoid repeating the same wording from the page body.

Do not create several FAQ questions with the same intent.

Do not create FAQ only to fill schema.

## 30. Headings

Headings should help the reader navigate.

Prefer practical headings such as:

- Kas tas ir?
- Kad to vajag?
- Kas to sagatavo?
- Ko iesniedz BIS?
- Kad drīkst sākt būvdarbus?
- Ko saņem beigās?
- Cik tas maksā?
- Cik ilgi tas aizņem?

Use exact user questions when they improve clarity.

Avoid generic headings such as:

- Papildu informācija;
- Svarīga informācija;
- Citi aspekti;
- Galvenie ieguvumi

unless the content genuinely requires them and a more exact heading is impossible.

## 31. Lists

Use lists when the content is genuinely list-like.

Do not force prose into bullets only for visual symmetry.

For document lists:

- one document per bullet;
- exact name;
- short explanation only if it changes understanding.

For process lists:

- one action per step;
- preserve real order;
- state actor when useful.

## 32. Tables

Use tables when the user needs to compare structured information.

Good uses:

- document vs who prepares vs who signs;
- situation vs required action;
- service scope vs price;
- before vs after regulatory change;
- role vs responsibility.

Do not use a table when normal prose is easier to understand.

## 33. Repetition

Avoid repeating:

- the same benefit;
- the same legal warning;
- the same service description;
- the same keyword;
- the same CTA.

If information must appear twice for UX reasons, keep the wording purposeful and short.

## 34. Approved terminology

Use the same verified professional term consistently.

Do not swap terms only for stylistic variety.

If the page uses one approved name for a document or role, preserve it across:

- body;
- FAQ;
- schema;
- internal links;
- captions;
- metadata where appropriate.

## 35. Historical or obsolete terms

If a term is obsolete but users still search for it:

- say clearly that the term is old;
- state the current term;
- explain whether the old document/process is still valid;
- give the date or rule change if verified.

Do not present obsolete terminology as current.

## 36. Changes after regulation updates

When regulation changed:

- state the exact effective date if verified;
- distinguish old and new procedure;
- explain what changes for the user;
- explain what remains valid for older projects if verified.

Do not write "after the changes" without naming the change or date when that distinction matters.

## 37. Client-facing service language

When ASTRO performs the work, write directly:

- sagatavojam;
- pārbaudām;
- iesniedzam;
- noformējam;
- aprēķinām;
- vadām procesu

only when ASTRO actually performs that action.

Do not use "koordinējam" to make direct execution sound broader.

Do not imply ASTRO performs regulated specialist work if it is actually performed by an external certified specialist.

State the real division of work when it matters to the client.

## 38. Marketing claims

Do not invent or exaggerate:

- years of experience;
- project count;
- client count;
- success rate;
- fastest turnaround;
- lowest price;
- market leadership;
- certificates;
- partner status.

Use only verified project facts.

If a claim can be replaced with a concrete fact, prefer the fact.

## 39. Images and captions

Image captions should explain what the user sees when that helps understanding.

Do not use empty captions such as:

- "Darba process";
- "Mūsu pieeja";
- "Profesionāls risinājums".

Prefer:

- "BIS būvdarbu žurnāla ieraksts";
- "Izpildmērījuma fragments";
- "Segto darbu akta parakstīšana BIS"

only when the image actually shows that content.

Alt text should describe the image’s useful meaning, not repeat SEO keywords.

## 40. Internal links

Internal-link anchor text should explain the destination.

Prefer exact anchors such as:

- būvdarbu žurnāls BIS;
- segto darbu akts;
- izpilddokumentācija;
- būves nodošana ekspluatācijā.

Avoid:

- lasīt vairāk;
- vairāk informācijas;
- spiediet šeit

when a descriptive anchor works.

## 41. CTA text

CTA should describe the next action.

Prefer:

- Pieteikt konsultāciju;
- Nosūtīt dokumentus pārbaudei;
- Saņemt izmaksu aprēķinu;
- Pārbaudīt, kas trūkst nodošanai

only when the destination and service actually match.

Avoid generic CTA text if a specific action is clearer.

## 42. Content review checklist

Before approving user-facing copy, check:

1. Is the first sentence useful?
2. Is the practical meaning clear?
3. Are exact roles named?
4. Are exact documents named?
5. Are exact actions named?
6. Are conditions explicit?
7. Are deadlines verified?
8. Are prices verified?
9. Is any wording vague?
10. Is any sentence filler?
11. Is any paragraph repeating another?
12. Is the language natural Latvian?
13. Can a non-professional understand it?
14. Does it sound like a real construction specialist?
15. Is the professional terminology correct?
16. Is the core answer easy to extract?
17. Did SEO wording make the text less natural?
18. Is any claim invented or unsupported?
19. Is any prohibited wording present?
20. Can anything be removed without losing information?

## 43. Final standard

The target is concise, technically correct and natural Latvian.

A good ASTRO text should feel like this:

- a competent construction specialist understands the subject;
- the client understands what to do;
- the exact document or action is clear;
- the text does not waste the reader’s time;
- the wording does not sound generated, bureaucratic or promotional.

When exact information is known, state it.

When information is not verified, say so.

Never fill a gap with a probable fact.
